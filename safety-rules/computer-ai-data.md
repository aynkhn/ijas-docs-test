# Computer, Artificial Intelligence, Data, and Programmable Device Safety
{: .no_toc }

1. TOC
{:toc}

## Why Do We Have These Rules?

Many science and engineering projects use computers, artificial intelligence (AI), programmable devices, sensors, robotics, and electronic datasets to collect, analyze, or interpret data. These are valuable research tools, but they can also create risks related to physical safety, eye strain, repetitive motion injuries, privacy, data security, and research integrity.

## Does My Project Involve These Hazards?

- using AI tools during any stage of the project;
- computer programming or software development;
- AI or machine learning;
- electronic or online datasets;
- internet-based data collection;
- programmable devices, including microcontrollers, single-board computers, robots, drones, or embedded systems;
- sensors or automated data collection systems; or
- extended computer use as part of the research process.

## Before You Begin Your Project

- understand how your software, AI tools, datasets, or programmable devices will be used;
- obtain permission to use software, datasets, and online resources when required;
- determine whether your project involves human participants or protected information;
- understand any legal, ethical, copyright, licensing, or privacy requirements that apply; and
- complete any required safety or endorsement forms.

## General Requirements

Students must:

- use computers, AI tools, datasets, and programmable devices responsibly and ethically;
- follow school policies and all applicable laws;
- accurately document the methods, software, AI tools, and datasets used;
- protect confidential or personally identifiable information;
- securely store research data; and
- immediately report damaged equipment, electrical hazards, or unsafe conditions to the supervising adult.

Students may **not**:

- access computers, networks, or online accounts without authorization;
- bypass security measures or attempt unauthorized access to computer systems;
- create or distribute malicious software or intentionally damage computer systems;
- falsify, fabricate, manipulate, or selectively alter research data or AI-generated results;
- violate copyright, software licenses, or terms of service; or
- present AI-generated work or analyses as entirely their own without appropriate disclosure.

## Computer and Ergonomic Safety

- maintain good posture while seated;
- position monitors to reduce neck strain and glare;
- take regular breaks to reduce eye strain and repetitive motion injuries;
- follow the **20-20-20 Rule**: look at an object at least 20 feet away for 20 seconds every 20 minutes of screen use;
- keep work areas organized to reduce tripping hazards from power cords and cables; and
- avoid eating or drinking near computers or electronic equipment.

## Electrical and Programmable Device Safety

- inspect equipment for damaged cords, batteries, or components before use;
- use equipment according to the manufacturer's instructions;
- disconnect power before modifying electrical circuits whenever possible;
- use only power supplies appropriate for the device;
- ensure moving parts do not create hazards for people or property; and
- operate drones or remotely controlled devices only where permitted and under appropriate adult supervision.

## Artificial Intelligence and Data

AI may be used as a research tool to assist with brainstorming, programming, data analysis, editing, image generation, or improving the clarity of writing. **Students remain responsible for the accuracy, integrity, originality, and scientific validity of all work submitted.**

Students using AI or electronic datasets must:

- independently verify important facts, calculations, references, code, analyses, and other AI-generated content;
- critically evaluate AI-generated information rather than accepting it without verification;
- clearly disclose how AI was used during the project, including its role in planning, coding, data analysis, writing, image generation, or other project activities;
- ensure that all conclusions are based on the student's own analysis and interpretation of the data;
- legally obtain datasets and comply with all copyright, licensing, and usage restrictions;
- properly cite the original sources of factual information identified through AI, rather than citing AI as the source of scientific facts; and
- comply with the [Human Participant Safety](human-participants) rules whenever human data are collected or analyzed.

{: .important }
> AI may assist with research but may **not** replace the student's own scientific reasoning, experimental design, data collection, analysis, or conclusions.

## AI Disclosure Statement

If AI tools were used during any stage of the project, students must include an AI Disclosure Statement in their paper or project documentation describing:

- the AI tool(s) used;
- how the AI tool(s) contributed to the project; and
- the steps taken to verify the accuracy of AI-generated information.
