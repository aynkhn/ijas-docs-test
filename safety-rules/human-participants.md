# Use and Care of Human Participants
{: .no_toc }

1. TOC
{:toc}

## Why Do We Have These Rules?

Research involving people can lead to new discoveries and help solve real-world problems. However, the safety, health, privacy, dignity, and rights of every participant must always come first.

Professional researchers receive specialized training in research ethics, and their studies are reviewed by Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) to protect participants. Although student research differs from professional research, IJAS follows these same ethical principles.

These rules apply whenever a project involves people in any way, including surveys, interviews, questionnaires, observations, testing products or prototypes, measuring physical or psychological responses, recording images or video, taste testing, or asking participants to perform any activity.

## Before You Begin Your Project

If your project involves human participants, you must:

- read these Human Participant Rules with your sponsor or teacher;
- determine whether SRC approval is required;
- prepare all surveys, interview questions, consent forms, and study materials before beginning your project;
- obtain all required approvals before recruiting participants or collecting data;
- complete the Human Participants Endorsement Form; and
- not begin any research until all required paperwork has been completed.

## General Rules for Human Participants

All human participant research must:

- present no greater than minimal risk than that encountered during everyday life or routine educational activities;
- obtain informed consent before participation;
- ensure participation is voluntary and allow participants to withdraw at any time without penalty;
- protect participant privacy by collecting only necessary information and maintaining confidentiality whenever possible;
- securely store identifiable information and report results without identifying participants whenever practical;
- minimize physical, emotional, psychological, social, legal, and economic risks; and
- not involve medical diagnosis, treatment, invasive procedures, bodily fluids, or administration of drugs or medical devices unless specifically permitted under separate IJAS rules.

## Informed Consent

Before participating, individuals — or a parent or legal guardian when required — must be informed of:

- the purpose of the study;
- what they will be asked to do;
- any reasonably foreseeable risks and expected benefits;
- how their information will be protected; and
- that participation is voluntary and they may withdraw at any time without penalty.

Photographs, audio recordings, and video recordings may only be collected with appropriate consent.

{: .note }
> A sample consent form is available with the endorsement forms.

## Behavioral Science Research

Behavioral science projects involve studying human thoughts, emotions, attitudes, opinions, decision-making, or social interactions.

{: .important }
> Studies that involve sensitive topics, deception, or the potential for emotional, psychological, or privacy-related harm require review by the IJAS Scientific Review Committee (SRC) **before** participants are recruited or data are collected.

Examples of sensitive topics include:

- physical or mental health;
- medical conditions or diagnoses;
- trauma, abuse, or neglect;
- family relationships, including divorce or family conflict;
- bullying, friendships, or social acceptance;
- body image or self-esteem;
- gender identity, sexual orientation, or sexual behavior;
- political beliefs or other strongly held personal beliefs;
- race, ethnicity, discrimination, or religion;
- alcohol, nicotine, vaping, cannabis, or illegal drug use;
- illegal activities; or
- any topic that could reasonably cause embarrassment, emotional distress, or invade a participant's privacy.

The procedure for obtaining SRC approval can be found with the endorsement forms.

## Physical Activity

Projects involving physical activity must present no greater than minimal risk. Students must:

- limit activities to those comparable to routine physical education classes or recreational activities;
- include evidence in the paper and on the display board that each participant has a current school physical examination on file with the school nurse, or written medical clearance from a licensed healthcare provider;
- provide appropriate adult supervision; and
- allow participants to stop the activity at any time without penalty.

## Food and Beverage Studies

Projects involving food or beverages must protect participants from allergic reactions, foodborne illness, and other avoidable health risks. Students must:

- use only foods and beverages that are safe for human consumption and prepared using appropriate food safety practices;
- include ingredients, amount of food or beverage, and preparation method on the informed consent form;
- include evidence in the paper and on the display board that quantities of food and non-alcoholic beverages are limited to normal serving amounts or less, and consumed in a reasonable amount of time;
- identify participant food allergies and dietary restrictions; and
- not administer alcohol, nicotine, cannabis, medications, or dietary supplements unless specifically permitted under separate IJAS rules.
