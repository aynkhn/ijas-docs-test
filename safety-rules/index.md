# Safety Rules — IJAS 2026-2028

Many science and engineering projects involve chemicals, equipment, electricity, heat, or other potential hazards. Following safe laboratory practices helps protect students, sponsors, judges, families, and the environment. Professional scientists receive safety training before working in laboratories. These rules are designed to help students learn and follow the same safe laboratory practices expected in schools, universities, and research facilities.

{: .warning }
> Projects that do not follow these safety rules may be disqualified from the Illinois Junior Academy of Science (IJAS) Science Fair.

## Before You Begin Your Project

Before beginning your project, you must:

1. Read the [General Laboratory Safety Rules](general-laboratory-safety) with your sponsor.
2. Read any additional safety sections that apply to your project (see table below).
3. Write your procedure.
4. Obtain any required approvals before beginning your research.
5. Complete the required Safety Sheet and any endorsement form(s).

{: .important }
> Do not begin your project until all required paperwork and approvals have been completed.

## Safety Assessment

Every project is different. Before you begin, think about the materials, equipment, and procedures you will use. Talk with your sponsor about any possible hazards and how you will prevent accidents. If your sponsor is unsure whether your project can be done safely, they should ask the Regional Safety Chair before beginning your research.

{: .note }
> Students and parents should **not** contact the Regional Safety Chair or State Safety Chair directly.

## Which Safety Rules Apply to My Project?

In addition to the [General Laboratory Safety Rules](general-laboratory-safety), read any section(s) below that apply to your project.

| If your project involves… | Read this section |
|---|---|
| Burning anything · Heating anything beyond room temperature · Chemicals (even common household chemicals) · Lasers, UV light, X-rays, or nuclear radiation | [Chemical, Fire, Heat, and Radiation Safety](chemical-fire-heat-radiation) |
| Computer programming · AI in any part of the project · Creating apps, robots, or other programmable devices · Large data sets not measured by the student | [Computer, AI, Data, and Programmable Device Safety](computer-ai-data) |
| Guns of any kind · Rockets · Fireworks · Explosives | [Firearms, Explosives, Rockets, and Projectile Safety](firearms-explosives-rockets) |
| Drones · Electrical devices · Motors · Engineered machinery · Tools · Vehicles (full-sized or models) | [Electrical, Drone, and Mechanical Safety](electrical-drone-mechanical) |
| Giving humans surveys or tests · Testing human reactions or senses · Humans eating/drinking, doing physical activity, or being tested · Observing people in public · Human mental health · Human testing of a device, app, game, or website | [Use and Care of Human Participants](human-participants) |
| Bacteria or viruses · Mold (even from bread or rotting food) · Fuel cells involving soil or bacteria · Water testing for bacteria/fecal contaminants · Compost · Insects, Daphnia, worms, or any invertebrate animal | [Use and Care of Biological Materials](biological-materials) |
