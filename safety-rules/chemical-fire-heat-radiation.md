# Chemical, Fire, Heat, and Radiation Safety
{: .no_toc }

1. TOC
{:toc}

## Why Do We Have These Rules?

Many science and engineering projects involve chemicals, heat, flames, lasers, ultraviolet (UV) light, or other forms of energy. When used properly, these materials and equipment can be handled safely — but they can also cause burns, fires, poisoning, eye injuries, radiation exposure, or other serious harm if used incorrectly.

## Does My Project Involve These Hazards?

- chemicals or chemical reactions;
- acids, bases, solvents, cleaning products, or laboratory reagents;
- fuels or combustible materials;
- heating or burning materials;
- open flames or hot equipment;
- lasers, UV light, infrared light, microwaves, or other non-ionizing radiation sources; or
- any procedure that could create a chemical, fire, heat, or radiation hazard.

## Before You Begin Your Project

- learn how to safely use all chemicals and equipment involved in your project;
- read the Safety Data Sheet (SDS), manufacturer's instructions, or another reliable reference for every chemical used;
- understand the hazards associated with your materials and equipment;
- plan how chemicals and waste will be safely stored and disposed of; and
- complete the required safety sheet and any required endorsement forms.

## General Safety Requirements

Students must:

- wear appropriate PPE, including safety glasses whenever there is a risk of eye injury;
- work only under the supervision required for the hazards involved;
- keep work areas clean and organized;
- know the location and proper use of emergency equipment (eyewash stations, safety showers, fire extinguishers, first aid supplies); and
- immediately report spills, injuries, equipment malfunctions, or unsafe conditions to the supervising adult.

Students may **not**:

- use explosive materials or chemicals, or intentionally create explosions;
- use known carcinogens, mutagens, teratogens, or illegal-drug precursor chemicals;
- obtain, possess, manufacture, or use prescription medications, controlled substances, illegal drugs, nicotine, or cannabis products (including THC, CBD, CBG);
- intentionally produce, distill, concentrate, or purify hazardous chemical products;
- use X-rays or radioactive materials; or
- conduct experiments that create unnecessary chemical, fire, heat, or radiation hazards.

{: .note }
> Fermentation with baker's yeast is covered under [Use and Care of Biological Materials](biological-materials). Any alcohol produced during permitted fermentation studies must remain incidental to the investigation and may not be distilled, concentrated, purified, or consumed.

## Chemical Safety

- use chemicals only for their intended purpose and according to instructions;
- clearly label all chemical containers;
- never mix chemicals unless the procedure specifically requires it;
- avoid inhaling vapors or allowing chemicals to contact skin or eyes;
- store incompatible chemicals separately;
- dispose of chemicals according to school, laboratory, or local regulations; and
- conduct experiments involving hazardous fumes or vapors in a fume hood or another appropriately ventilated area.

## Fire Safety

- keep flammable materials away from ignition sources;
- conduct burning procedures in a fume hood or another well-ventilated area that safely exhausts smoke and combustion products;
- use open flames only when necessary and under appropriate adult supervision;
- never leave an open flame unattended;
- secure loose clothing and tie back long hair;
- know how to extinguish fires safely; and
- have appropriate fire safety equipment readily available before beginning the experiment.

## Heat Safety

- use heat-resistant gloves or appropriate tools when handling hot objects;
- assume heated equipment remains hot until it has cooled completely;
- place hot equipment on heat-resistant surfaces;
- avoid rapid temperature changes that could cause glassware to break; and
- allow equipment to cool before storing or cleaning it.

## Radiation and Light Source Safety

{: .important }
> Projects involving X-rays or radioactive materials are **not permitted**. Only commercially manufactured Class 1, Class 2, or Class 3R lasers may be used — high-powered Class 3B and Class 4 lasers are not permitted.

Students must:

- use radiation-producing equipment only when appropriate for the project and under qualified adult supervision;
- follow all manufacturer instructions and institutional safety requirements;
- use appropriate shielding, barriers, or enclosures when required;
- never look directly into a laser beam or aim a laser at any person or reflective surface;
- wear protective eyewear appropriate for the wavelength of the laser or UV source whenever there is a risk of eye exposure;
- wear clothing that protects exposed skin when working with UV light; and
- never intentionally expose themselves or others to lasers, UV light, infrared radiation, or other radiation sources.

## Storage and Disposal

- store chemicals according to the manufacturer's instructions or the SDS;
- keep incompatible chemicals separated;
- clearly label all containers;
- dispose of chemicals according to school procedures and all applicable regulations; and
- never pour hazardous chemicals down a sink or place them in regular trash unless specifically permitted by your school or laboratory.
